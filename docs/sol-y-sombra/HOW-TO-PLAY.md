# Sol y Sombra: how to play

You are the matador for one afternoon. Make passes with the cape to win over the crowd, without letting the horns catch you, then go to the moment of truth. The crowd's mood decides your reward. Press **Enter the ring** on the game menu to start; the afternoon opens with the paseo and the bull's entrance, which you can skip.

## Controls

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `1` `2` `3` | The cards | Choose a cape move (veronica, outside pass, swirl); at the moment of truth, a finish; when you're down, get up or retire |
| `K` | The moment of truth card | Go to the moment of truth (after only one or two passes, the game asks first) |
| `Space` | Skip ahead | Skip to the end of what's playing (the paseo, a pass, a walk) |
| `Enter` | Continue | After the crowd's roll, see what it gives |
| `R` | Another afternoon | On the results, a new bull |
| `H` | Back to the Hall | On the results |

Esc, the pause button and the Keyring shortcut work as in every Any Key game.

## The rules

**The bull.** Its bill shows its quality, from Magnificent (dangerous, and the crowd credits you more for it) to Meek (safe, and worth little to the crowd), and two habits that change the odds: hooking to one side, charging late, tiring early, following the cloth rather than the man, keeping its strength for the end.

**The crews.** Your picadores and banderilleros work the bull first. Their card says how good their work was and what it did: good work makes your passes safer, poor work riskier.

**Passes.** Each pass, choose one of three moves:

| Move | Risk | What the crowd thinks |
|---|---|---|
| Veronica | The riskiest | The most admired: bravery and artistry |
| Outside pass | Middling | Less daring |
| Swirl | The safest | Showy, but not brave |

The cards show the real chance of a goring and of a close call for the pass you're about to make, and what the crowd will add if it goes well. The odds change with every pass: the bull tires, so later passes are safer; passes go by alternate sides, so a bull that hooks one way makes every other pass riskier. The crowd tires of the same move three times running.

**A close call** is a clean pass with the horns brushing the suit: the crowd adds bravery and artistry for it.

**Gored.** The horn throws you onto the sand and your crew run in to draw the bull away. You choose:

- **Get up and carry on**: the crowd adds courage, and from now on every goring chance is a third higher.
- **Retire**: you're helped off, the crowd takes courage away, and the afternoon ends with applause at most.

A serious goring ends the afternoon: you're helped to the infirmary (you will be fine), and the crowd's sympathy adds a little.

**The moment of truth.** When you're ready, press `K`. Two ways to finish:

| Finish | Chance | If it works |
|---|---|---|
| Over the horns | Steadier: better with each pass you made first | The crowd adds a little |
| Waiting for the charge | Riskier, and a miss is more likely to gore | The crowd adds a lot, and rises for it |

A miss costs the crowd's goodwill and you try again; after three misses the bull is led back to the pens. When a finish works, the bull goes down onto the sand to rest. It gets up later and walks back to the pens.

## Modes

| Mode | What it is |
|---|---|
| Classic, Corrida | Choose each pass from the cards and watch it play out; the rules above |
| 1970s mode | The 1978 program on a terminal, question for question: type `YES` or `NO` in capitals, cape moves `0` `1` `2`, and `4` or `5` for the kill. It keeps every quirk of the original, in our own words |

Arena (real time) and Career (a season), and the Course Camarguaise and Recortes traditions, are on their way.

## Settings

| Setting | What it changes |
|---|---|
| Play style, Tradition, Style | Classic, Corrida and the Vintage Poster for now; the others are shown and arrive later |
| Slow motion at the closest moment | Each pass slows as the horns go by |
| The stands | Full or half full; half draws faster on a slow machine |
| Ring sounds | The crowd, the hooves, the cape and the band (the switch in the top bar silences everything) |
| Motion | The collection's setting: reduced motion keeps the crowd still, turns off slow motion and cuts the long walks short |
| Forget my data | Records and keycaps for this game |

## Scoring

The crowd keeps a meter, shown top right:

| Part | Comes from |
|---|---|
| Bravery | The moves you make, more for the veronica; close calls |
| Artistry | Clean passes, less when you repeat a move three times running |
| The bull | A finer bull is worth more |
| Risk taken | The goring chances you accepted |
| The finish | A finish that works; less for each miss |
| Courage | Getting up after a goring (more), retiring (less), dragging past ten passes |

At the end the crowd rolls once: the needle lands somewhere within 3.5 either side of the meter, and the zone it lands in is the award. Applause starts at 8, a lap of honour at 15, the shoulders at 22. Without a finish that works, applause is the most the crowd will give. The dial shows each award's chance before it rolls.

## Achievements

| Keycap | Tier | How to earn it |
|---|---|---|
| First Paseo | Common | See an afternoon through to the crowd's verdict |
| Seven Passes | Common | Make seven passes before the moment of truth |
| Close Call | Common | Let the horns brush the suit and come through it |
| Back on Your Feet | Rare | Get up after a goring and carry on |
| All Veronicas | Rare | Five passes or more, every one a veronica, and a finish that works |
| Lap of Honour | Rare | Have the crowd call you round the ring |
| On Their Shoulders | Legendary | Leave through the great gate, carried |
| Secret | Rare | Something the 1978 instructions got backwards |

## Tips

1. A magnificent bull is dangerous but worth it: the crowd starts warm.
2. Read the habits. Against a bull that hooks left, make your bravest passes when the card says "by his right".
3. Each pass makes the finish likelier. Four or five passes before the moment of truth is rarely wasted.
4. A swirl or two keeps you safe and keeps the crowd from tiring of your veronicas.
5. Waiting for the charge is worth twice as much to the crowd as going over the horns, and a miss is far more likely to gore. Take it when the card's number is good.
6. The shoulders need a finish that works and a crowd above 22: bravery, close calls and a fine bull get you there.
