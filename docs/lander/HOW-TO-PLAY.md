# Don't Crater: how to play

You are falling towards a world at 5,000 km/h and your only control is the engine. Brake too early and you run out of fuel high up; brake too late and you dig a crater. Touch down under 20 km/h to walk away, and under 5 km/h to climb out and plant your own flag. Pick a world and a mode on the game menu. Every flight except 1979 mode opens on a ready card with the keys for that mode, and the first press of `Space` (or a tap on the scene) starts the descent.

## Controls

Esc, the Pause button, `R` and `H` on the results, `K` for the Keyring and `?` for the shortcuts sheet work as in every Any Key game. These are the keys Don't Crater adds.

### Flying (Live, missions, Daily Drop and Free Flight)

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `Space` or `Enter` | Start the descent button, or tap the scene | Starts the descent from the ready card |
| Hold `Space` | Hold the orange BURN button | Full power for as long as you hold it |
| `↑` / `↓` | Drag the throttle lever | Throttle up or down by 5; with `Shift`, by 1 |
| `W` / `S` | | Same as `↑` / `↓` (not in Free Flight, where `S` is the scan) |
| `Page Up` / `Page Down` | | Throttle up or down by 20 |
| `0` to `9` | | Throttle to 0, 10, 20 … 90 |
| `F` | | Full throttle (100) |
| `X` | | Cut the engine (0) |
| `T` | Warp button | Time warp: automatic, or off |
| `G` | Guide button | Burn guide on or off |
| `A` | Assist button | Landing assist on or off |
| `P` or `Esc` | Pause button in the top bar | Pause and resume |
| `R` | Restart flight, in the pause menu | Starts this flight again at once, even mid-flight |

On missions that switch the guide off (Instruments Out and Express Delivery), `G` and `A` do nothing and their buttons are hidden.

### Ten-Second mode

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `↑` / `↓` (`Shift` for 1) | The slider, or the − and + buttons | Sets the burn for the next ten seconds |
| `0` to `9`, `F`, `X`, `Page Up` / `Page Down` | | Set the burn, as in Live |
| `Enter` or `Space` | The "Burn … for 10 s" button | Plays the next ten seconds (the first one starts the flight) |

Ten-Second mode has no time warp and no hold-to-burn: each turn plays at the burn you set.

### Free Flight adds

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hold `←` / `→` | Hold the rotate buttons at the bottom left | Lean left or right at 60° a second; with `Shift`, 20° a second. The lean stays when you let go |
| Hold `U` | Hold both rotate buttons | Level the lander (about half a second from a big lean) |
| Hold `Q` / `E` | Hold the jet buttons above the rotate buttons | Side jets: push left or right at 8 km/h a second, at once and without leaning. They burn fuel from the main tank |
| `S` | Scan button | Scan assist on or off (remembered for next time) |

`←`, `→`, `Q` and `E` also start the descent from the ready card.

### On the game menu

`Esc` closes an open panel (Missions, Free Flight or the flag designer); with none open, it goes back to the Hall. In the Free Flight panel, type a site number and press `Enter` to fly it.

## The rules

1. **You start high and fast.** Every flight begins far above the world (212 km over the Moon, 521 km over Earth), falling straight down at 5,000 km/h. Missions and the Daily Drop change the entry speed, the fuel and the cargo; Free Flight's final approach starts only 3 km up.
2. **The engine pushes against the fall.** Throttle from 0 to 100. A bigger throttle brakes harder and burns fuel faster. The lander gets lighter as the tank empties, so the same throttle brakes harder near the end than at the start.
3. **The speed follows the push.** Cut the engine and the braking stops at once, but the speed doesn't. A lander you have pushed upwards keeps rising until gravity has used that speed up, like a ball thrown in the air. The instruments show whether your speed is growing or shrinking, and a chip beside a climbing lander says when the climb will top out.
4. **Fuel is the whole game.** Fall freely for as long as you dare, then brake hard so that you stop right at the ground. Burn too early and the tank runs dry high up; then nothing can stop the fall.
5. **The touchdown speed decides everything.** Under 20 km/h is a landing; under 5 km/h your astronaut climbs out and plants your flag; under 2 km/h is a perfect landing. Anything faster is a crash, from a dented lander to a brand new crater. Hit at 5,000 km/h or more and you strike oil.
6. **Up is not an escape.** On Psyche, gravity is so weak that a lander climbing past 222 km is lost in space.

The world's gravity changes everything: Earth pulls six times harder than the Moon and needs a long, hard brake, while Psyche barely pulls at all and asks for a feather touch.

## Modes

| Mode | What changes | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **Live** | Real time with a throttle lever and automatic time warp | Everyone; start here |
| **Ten-Second** | The 1979 rhythm: set a burn, watch ten seconds play out, choose again. A dotted path and a ghost lander show where the burn takes you | Planners |
| **Missions** | Ten landings in order, each with a twist | Players who want goals and stars |
| **Daily Drop** | One descent a day, the same for everyone | A quick daily habit |
| **Free Flight** | The lander leans and drifts sideways and must stand upright on flat ground among rocks | Pilots who have mastered the drop |
| **1979 mode** | The original program, turn for turn, on a blocky black-and-white screen | The curious |

Live, Ten-Second and a new Free Flight site fly to the world picked on the game menu: the Moon, Mars, Psyche (a real metal asteroid) or Earth, where you land on a barge at sea. Missions, the Daily Drop and the named Free Flight sites choose their own world.

### Live

The game speeds up time (up to sixteen times) while nothing can happen soon, and slows down as a decision comes near. The last 2.5 km are flown in real time, and the last 30 m play at half speed, which gives you twice as long to react. With an empty tank and a long way to fall, it fast-forwards to the ending. The clock shows the warp. `T` turns it off.

### Ten-Second

Each turn you set a burn and press `Enter`. Before you commit, the panel says where the turn will leave you, and whether a full burn after that would still stop you above the ground. If the turn will touch down, it says at what speed.

### Missions

| 2 | Red Planet | Mars | None |
| 3 | Metal World | Psyche | None, but gravity is almost nothing |
| 4 | Homecoming | Earth | None, but the longest fall |
| 5 | Cargo Run | Moon | Three tonnes of cargo on board |
| 6 | Running on Fumes | Mars | Three quarters of a tank |

### Free Flight

Open the Free Flight panel on the game menu and choose a start:

- **Final approach:** 3 km above the ground, already slow, with a sideways drift. A round lasts a minute or two.
- **Full descent:** the world's usual height and 5,000 km/h, drifting sideways at 300 to 650 km/h.

Every flight has a planned landing site, marked **LAND HERE**. A strip across the top shows the whole site in profile: where you are, which way you drift, the target, and where your path meets the ground. A bracket on the ground marks the same spot in the scene.

To stand, the lander needs:

- ground sloping 8° or less under both feet (the feet are 8 m apart);
- a lean within 10° of the ground's slope;
- a sideways slide of 6 km/h or less (relative to the deck, on a drifting barge);
- a vertical speed under 20 km/h, as everywhere.

There is no air out there to slow a drift: once you are sliding sideways you keep sliding until something pushes the other way. Two things can. Leaning tips the main engine's push sideways, which is strong but slow to set up, and while you hold your height it gives only part of the engine's push (on the Moon a 20° lean takes about 25 seconds to stop a 40 km/h drift). The **side jets** push straight sideways the moment you press `Q` or `E`, on every world at the same 8 km/h a second, so the same drift dies in 5 seconds and 31 m, upright. They burn as much fuel as the main engine would for the same push, so big drifts are still cheapest to kill by leaning during the main burn.

Come down gently but break one of those rules and the lander tips over; on Earth, miss the barges and it splashes into the sea. The result card says what went wrong. Any flat ground saves you, but only the target earns all three stars.

**Unlimited fuel.** Choose **Unlimited** on the fuel row to fly Free Flight without the worry. You get the Standard tank, and whenever it runs dry it is filled again at once, with a gush and a banner ("Tank refilled · refill 2"); the engine never misses a beat. The HUD counts the refills under the fuel bar. The result card shows **Refills** in place of the fuel left, and how many tanks the flight used ("1.5 tanks", or "52% of a tank" without a refill) against your best for that world and start; a standing landing that uses less sets a new best, so you can watch yourself need fewer refills. Stars, named sites and keycaps count as usual, and the share text says how many refills it took. Missions, the Daily Drop and 1979 mode never offer it.

**New site** builds a site from a random number, shown on screen and in the share text. Type a number into **Site #** and press **Fly** (or `Enter`) to fly that site again. Six **named sites** unlock in order, one star on a site opening the next, for 18 stars in all:

| Site | World | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| The Marbles | Moon | A boulder field with few, small clearings |
| Night Rim | Moon | A crater rim, always by Earthshine |
| The Brink | Mars | A canyon edge with a narrow ledge halfway down |
| Blue Hour Dunes | Mars | Dunes at dusk; only the sand between them is level |
| Iron Crags | Psyche | Metal spires with a few shelves |
| Barge Row | Earth | Barges on the swell, one of them drifting |

In the last 60 m a second advice line handles the sideways part: "aim 0 sideways · jets ◀ Q" for a drift the jets can kill in a few seconds, "aim 0 sideways · tilt 12° left" for a bigger one, and "stand up (U)" once the drift is gone.

### 1979 mode

The original program's numbers and turns, on a model of a 1979 home computer screen. The words and pictures are new.

1. Answer `1` if you want the instructions, anything else to skip them.
2. Choose a world: `1` Earth, `2` the Moon, `3` Mars, `4` an asteroid.
3. Each turn, type a burn from 0 to 100 and press `Enter`. Decimals work. A burn outside 0 to 100 is refused; text gets `?REDO`; `Enter` alone repeats your last burn. The panel shows the time, height, speed and fuel; a minus speed means you are going up.
4. Below 1 km the lander steps down the screen, and near the ground it kicks up dust.
5. At the end you see the verdict, your impact speed and the time, and are asked whether to go again: `1` for yes, `2` for no. After a no, type `RUN` at the prompt to start over.

Like the original, the clock keeps counting from one game to the next, and a landing between 19 and 20 km/h shows a wreck while the text says you landed. 1979 mode always uses the original tanks: the fuel setting doesn't apply.

**On the game menu**, remembered in this browser:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Fuel**: Generous, Standard, Tight or Unlimited | Generous carries 30% more fuel. Standard is the tank every mode was balanced on. Tight leaves a perfect run only about 7% to spare (Free Flight has its own tight tanks). Every mode but 1979 mode; a non-Standard setting shows on the ready card and in the share text. **Unlimited** is for Free Flight only: see below. Every other mode flies the Standard tank while it is chosen |
| **Burn guide** (on at first) | A dashed line at the stop point: where a full burn started now would stop you. Green while safe, amber when close, red "CAN'T STOP" when too late. The instruments count down ("burn in 0:34", then "stops at 2.1 km" while braking) |
| **Landing assist** (off at first) | Two lines to fly by. Keep the lander above the green line and braking at about 85% brings you down under 5 km/h; below the amber line even full power can't get you under 20. Close to the ground a chip shows what holding your throttle would do. Flights with it are marked in the share text |
| **Free Flight start** | Final approach or Full descent |
| **Scan assist** (Free Flight, off at first) | Shades the ground within 1.5 km green where it is flat enough to stand on (4° or less), amber where it is borderline and red where it is too steep. Flights with it are marked in the share text and on the recorder |

`G`, `A` and `T` in flight change only the current flight; `S` is remembered. With the guide or the assist on, the last stretch gets a target speed and a throttle to brake with, rounded for the number keys: "aim 9 km/h · brake (50%)".

**Forget my data** at the bottom of the game menu clears Don't Crater's progress, its keycaps and its Daily Drops from this browser, after asking. Your flag stays.

The result card shows the ending, your impact speed, the fuel left and the flight time (the number of turns in Ten-Second mode), a flight recorder and one piece of advice. - **Missions** keep your best stars for each mission.
- **Free Flight:** standing on the target earns the landing's stars; standing anywhere else is a diversion and earns one star at most. The card says how far off you came down. On a named site, two precision bonuses are kept: a **narrow ledge** (a flat under 20 m wide) and **dead centre** (within 1.5 m of the middle, or 8% of the flat's width). The Free Flight recorder adds your distance from the target, against the autopilot's flight, with your tilt as a second strip.
- **The game menu** shows your best touchdown on each world, a flag mark where you have planted it, and a flight log of flights, landings and craters.

## Daily

The **Daily Drop** changes at midnight on your own clock. Number 1 was 1 January 2026, so 7 October 2026 is Daily Drop #280. Everyone gets the same world, entry speed (4,200 to 6,000 km/h), fuel load (86% to 112% of the world's tank) and cargo (up to six tonnes) on the same day, and every drop is checked so that a perfect run keeps at least 6% of the tank. Your fuel setting still applies, and shows in the share text, so compare results at the same setting.

Your **first flight of the day counts**, landing or crash. After it, the game menu shows your result and you can keep practising. A flown Daily Drop counts towards your daily streak on the Keyring.

The share text has three lines:

1. "Don't Crater · Daily Drop #280".
2. A mark for the world and its name; a mark for your ending and your touchdown speed; a fuel pump and the fuel left. A non-Standard fuel setting and the landing assist each add a mark.
3. Eight squares, one for each eighth of your flight: dark for coasting, orange for a partial burn, a flame for full power. Your ending's mark closes the line.

Any other flight shares the same way, with the mode and the world in the first line. A Free Flight share names the site, says whether you landed on target or how far off, and marks the scan assist.

## Tips

1. **Fall first.** Full power from the start stops you 136 km above the Moon, and the climb back up wastes the tank. Coast, and burn when the stop point touches the ground.
2. **Use the number keys.** `0` to coast, `F` or a held `Space` to brake, then the advised figure in the final approach. The lever is there for fine trims.
3. **Use the slow last 30 m.** Time runs at half speed there. Aim for the target speed the advice gives you, not for zero.
4. **On Psyche, think small.** Hovering takes under 1% of throttle, so tap rather than hold, and use `Shift` with the arrows.
5. **Kill the drift while you brake.** In Free Flight, lean against the drift during the main burn, when the engine is running anyway; fixing it later costs far more fuel. Trim the last few km/h with the side jets (`Q`, `E`): they need no lean, so you can stay upright over the flat. Stand up with `U` in the last few metres.
6. **Remember Red Planet.** On Instruments Out the speed reading is dead above 40 km. A perfect run on Mars starts braking at about 130 km.
7. **In 1979 mode, decimals are allowed.** The asteroid needs a burn of about 0.7 to hover; 1 is already too much.
