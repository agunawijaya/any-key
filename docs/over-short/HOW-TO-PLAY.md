# Over / Short: how to play

You lay a field gun on a target you cannot see. Set an elevation, fire, and the observer near the target telephones
back where the round came down: over (beyond the target) or short (in front of it), and usually by how much. Use
that to correct the next round, until one lands close enough to destroy the target.

The game menu offers six ways to play, two to a row; the one you played last is first:

| Way to play | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Classic** | The 1973 drill: five targets, five rounds each, the 18-round standard |
| **Campaign** | Twenty missions in five chapters, each breaking the 1973 formula a little more |
| **Field Artillery** | The full-physics game: charge, elevation and line, wind across the line of fire, real ground, an observer who gives corrections |
| **Counter-Battery Duel** | Two guns either side of a ridge, against a friend on the same screen or a computer commander |
| **Daily Barrage** | One Field Artillery shoot a day, the same for everyone |
| **The 1970s** | Gunner (1973) and Cannoneer (IBM PC) on their own terminals, as they ran |

A briefing says what each game is about, and **Take the gun** (or Enter) starts it.

On the 1970s terminals, type at the prompt and press Enter, as in 1973. While the teletype is printing, any key
finishes the line at once.

Esc pauses, as in every game in the collection. The pause menu also has **New game** (Classic), **Restart the
mission** (the Campaign and Field Artillery), **Restart the Daily Barrage**, **A new duel**, or **Load the program
again** (the terminals).

## The rules of Classic and the Campaign

- **Elevation** is the barrel's angle above the ground, from 1° to 89° (some missions allow less). In still air
  45° throws furthest; every distance short of the greatest can be reached at two elevations, one either side of 45°.
- **The fall of shot.** The round lands somewhere on the line from the gun through the target. The observer calls it
  **over** if it fell beyond the target and **short** if it fell before it, and in most games says by how much,
  rounded (to the metre in Classic, to ten metres in the Campaign).
- **A hit** is any round that comes down within the target's radius: 100 m in Classic, from 100 to 150 m in the
  Campaign (the briefing says). A hit destroys most targets; a stone bridge takes two.
- **Rounds.** Classic gives five rounds at each target; miss with all five and the enemy finds your gun first, which
  ends the game. A Campaign mission has a number of rounds for the whole mission; run out and the mission is lost.
- **The observer's map** plots each reported round as a numbered dot, over to the right of the target and short to
  the left. When the observer gives only a direction, those rounds are listed under the map instead, since no
  distance was given.

## Modes

### Classic

The 1973 drill. The card tells you the gun's greatest range and the target's distance. Five targets, one after
another, each with five rounds. Destroy all five in **18 rounds or fewer** and you have met the standard; more, and
you are sent back to the artillery school for a refresher.

### Campaign

Twenty missions in five chapters. A chapter opens when the last mission of the chapter before it has been won;
inside an open chapter, play the missions in any order.

| Chapter | What changes | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Drill | The observer says only "over" or "short", never how far | Bracketing: one over, one short, then halve the difference |
| 2. Unknown Gun | Nobody gives you the gun's greatest range, or the figure you are given is wrong | Finding a gun's range from its own fall of shot |
| 3. Hills and Wind | Targets high on a ridge or down in a valley; a strong wind with or against the shell | The formula is only a place to start |
| 4. Real Air | The shells fly through air, which slows them: the furthest elevation is now below 45°, and the two roads no longer meet | Building your own range table |
| 5. Pressure | A convoy on the move, three rounds left, a clock, and an enemy battery hunting your gun | Speed and nerve |

- **Moving targets** are judged where they are when the round comes down. A round can take half a minute to arrive:
  aim where the convoy will be.
- **Against the clock**, a round that lands after time is up does not count. A high round flies for much longer than
  a low one.
- **Counter-battery.** After each of your turns the enemy fires at your gun, and each of their rounds lands closer
  than the last; the card shows how close the last one came. **Move the gun back** costs a turn, but they must find
  you again. From the new pit the target is that much further away.

### Field Artillery

The gun as a real battery laid it. Three things set where a round goes:

- **The charge.** Four bags of propellant, from charge 1 (about 3.3 km at most) to charge 4 (about 9.9 km). A
  small charge at a steep angle drops over a crest; a big one at a low angle arrives sooner.
- **The elevation**, from 0° to 70°.
- **The line**, in mils either side of the target's bearing (6,400 mils make a circle; at 5 km a mil is about 5 m),
  up to 300 either way.

The rounds fly through real air over real ground. Wind blows along and across the line of fire and gusts from round
to round, so rounds drift sideways, and the longer a round is in the air the further it drifts. The target card
gives the distance as the map has it and how far the target stands above or below the gun; the forecast gives the
wind. Neither is quite right, this barrel throws a little differently from its table, and no two rounds are quite
the same: the first round rarely hits, the way it rarely did for a real battery.

**The observer** answers with the correction to make, in their own words: "Add 200. Left 50." means the round fell
200 m short and 50 m right, so add 200 m and come 50 m left. The **map** (`M`) shows every round from above, with
left and right; the **firing table** (`T`) gives, for the charge in the breech, the range at each elevation over
level ground in still air, the time of flight, the highest point, and what 1 m/s of wind across and behind does.

**Time.** Laying and loading takes 25 seconds of game time between rounds, 15 more if you change the charge, and
then the round's own flight. The clock matters when a column is on its way to a bridge, when an enemy battery is
firing, and for medals.

The ten shoots, all open from the start:

| Shoot | What it asks of you |
|---|---|
| F1 Registration | Charge, elevation and line on a quiet morning |
| F2 Behind the Crest | The target sits behind a crest: a flat round buries itself in the hillside; a small charge at a steep angle falls over the top |
| F3 Crosswind | A strong, gusting wind across the line: lay off the wind |
| F4 The Supply Road | A convoy crossing the line of fire: aim ahead of it along the road |
| F5 Smoke | Twice the observer loses a round in the enemy's smoke: keep your own count |
| F6 Counter-Battery | The target is a battery already ranging on you: silence it, or move |
| F7 Drop the Bridge | Two hits on a bridge before the column reaches it; a round still in the air when it crosses is too late |
| F8 Night Shoot | In the dark the observer sees only the flash: over or short, left or right, no distances. Three star shells light the target for about a minute each |
| F9 Bridge by Night | The bridge, the clock and the dark together |
| F10 Under Fire | A target to destroy while a battery out of your reach hunts your gun |

- **Enemy fire.** A battery hunting your gun fires every so often (every 75, 55 or 44 seconds by difficulty). Its
  first round at a new pit lands about 700 m off, each next one closer; the HUD says how close the last came. One
  that lands within 40 m knocks the gun out and the shoot is lost. **Move the gun back** (`R`) takes the gun 400 m
  down the road in two and a half minutes, at most twice: their next rounds fall on the empty pit, and they must find
  you all over again. From the new pit, the target is 400 m further away.
- **Smoke** hides the fall of the second and the fourth round at the target from the observer.
- **Star shells** (`L`) take ten seconds to light the target and burn for about seventy. While one burns, the
  observer gives corrections in metres; in the dark, only the flash.
- **Medals** go by rounds and game time together: the briefing gives both for **gold** and **silver**; any win is
  **bronze**. The best medal on each shoot is kept for each difficulty.

### Counter-Battery Duel

Two guns either side of a ridge, neither in sight of the other, each with an observer on the crest. You command the
west gun. Each turn both guns are laid in secret and fire together; whichever round comes down first is judged
first, so a quick, flat round can win a turn a slow lob would have won too late. **Two hits** within 25 m silence a
battery. Instead of firing, a battery may **move back** 400 m (twice a duel): the other side's round falls on the
empty pit and their observer has to find the gun again. Each battery has 20 rounds; if both run dry, the one that
took fewer hits has won.

Pick your opponent on the setup screen:

| Opponent | How it fights |
|---|---|
| A friend, on this screen | Hot seat: you take turns at the gun. Between turns a screen hides everything until the next commander presses Enter, so neither sees the other's orders or reports |
| The cautious commander | Trusts only part of each report, and moves the gun as soon as your rounds come close |
| The aggressive commander | Never moves, loads the biggest charge so its rounds arrive first, and over-corrects |
| The methodical commander | Follows its observer to the letter, and moves only when a round lands right beside it |

### Daily Barrage

One Field Artillery shoot a day, the same for everyone, on Normal: the day picks the shoot, the ground, the target
and the wind, and on some days a battery further back joins in and hunts your gun. Each run of ten days brings all
ten shoots, in an order of its own. Your first finish of the day is the one kept.

**Share result** copies three lines, or opens the share sheet on a touch screen: "Over / Short · Daily Barrage" with
the day's number; a row of marks, one a round (an arrow up for over, down for short, sideways for a round on the
range but off the line, a cloud for one lost in smoke, a bullseye for a hit that leaves the target standing, a
burst for the one that brings it down), followed by the result ("target down
in 4 rounds") and the day's wind ("wind 12 km/h from the west"); and the link to the game.

### The 1970s

The two programs behind Over / Short, played turn for turn on terminals of their day:

- **Gunner, 1973**: a teletype, in yards and in capitals, printing at the speed of the machine.
- **Cannoneer, IBM PC**: a green screen in metres, with its PC speaker: a report, a rising whistle and a thud for
  every round.

Everything the programs did is kept, quirks included: the textbook formula (so ½ · asin(distance ÷ greatest range)
hits every time), fractions of a degree, a round exactly 100 short called **over**, elevations outside 1 to 89
refused at no cost, the sixth elevation at one target never fired because the enemy finds the gun first, and the
verdict on 18 rounds. Both programs fight the same war every time they are loaded, as they did. Gunner only plays
again on a capital `Y`; Cannoneer takes `y` as well. The words on screen are our own.

The game's own settings are under **Game settings** in the game menu. Sound, light and dark, and motion are in the

## Scoring

- **Classic** counts the rounds you fire in all. Meeting the 18-round standard is the aim; your best total is kept.
- **Campaign** missions earn a medal by the rounds they took: the briefing shows how many for **gold** and
  **silver**; any win is **bronze**. Your best medal and fewest rounds for each mission are kept.
- **Field Artillery** shoots earn a medal by rounds and game time, kept per difficulty with your best rounds and time.
- **The duel** keeps your wins, draws and hot-seat games, and which commanders you have beaten.
- **The Daily Barrage** keeps each day's result and marks.
- **The 1970s** keep the wars begun and finished on each terminal, and your fewest rounds.

## Tips

1. Bracket first. One round clearly over and one clearly short tell you more than two near misses on the same side.
2. Halve the difference between the last over and the last short; when the observer gives distances, split it in
   proportion instead: after a round 300 over and one 100 short, the right elevation lies a quarter of the way from
   the short one's to the over one's.
3. Near the greatest range, a degree changes very little; close in at low elevations, a tenth of a degree matters.
4. The second road is free: in still air, 90° minus your elevation lands in the same place, which is handy when a
   ridge is in the way or a bridge needs a second hit.
5. Read the firing table. Two rows either side of the distance you want give the elevation better than any formula
   once air, hills and wind are in play.
6. Against a moving target, take the time of flight from the table and lead the convoy by its speed times that time.
7. In Field Artillery, the observer's correction is the whole message: if they say "Add 150", the round fell 150 m
   short, whatever the map said. Your first round tells you how far off the map, the forecast and the barrel are
   together, and that error carries on to the next round.
8. Lay off the wind before the first round: the table's last two columns give the drift per m/s of wind. A gust
   still moves rounds about, so expect the line to need a correction or two.
9. Behind a crest, check the flight on screen: a round that hits the hillside is reported short by a long way. Drop
   a charge and raise the elevation.
10. Keep a star shell for the rounds that matter: one burning while two rounds land is worth twice as much.
