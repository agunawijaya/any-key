# Same Trick Twice: how to play

Sow your seeds round the board so that more of them end up in your store than in your opponent's. Pick a game from the
game menu (the Learner is first), and you always sow first. The row of six pits nearest you is yours, and your store is
the long hollow on the right.

## Controls

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `1`–`6` | Click a pit | Sow from that pit (your pits, left to right) |
| `←` `→`, then `Enter` | | Choose a pit, then sow it |
| | Drag from a pit | Pick its seeds up; pass over each next pit to drop one there; let go to sow the rest |
| | Drag back onto the same pit | Put the seeds back, no move made |
| `Space` | | Skip the sowing: every seed lands at once |
| `Z` | Take back | Undo your last move (against the Scholar or a friend only) |
| `B` | Memory Book | Open the Learner's book (games against the Learner) |

Pointing at a pit shows where its seeds will go: a dot in each pit on the way, a ring where the last one lands, and
rings round any pit it would capture. The numbers by the pits are off unless you turn them on (Game settings), but a
pit always shows its number while you point at it. The two stores' totals always show at the ends of the board.

## The rules

Every rule set shares the same moves: lift **all** the seeds from one of your pits that has any, and sow them one at a
time into the holes that follow, going right along your row, then left along your opponent's. What happens next
depends on the rules.

### 1978 Awari

- Three seeds in every pit to start.
- The sowing goes through **both** stores, so a long sowing can drop a seed in your opponent's store.
- If your last seed lands in **your store**, you sow again, but only once: a second store landing passes the turn.
- If your last seed lands in an **empty pit**, on either side, and the pit straight across has seeds, you capture them
  and the last seed into your store. Landing in an empty pit on your opponent's side captures your own pit across.
- The game ends **the moment either row is empty**. Seeds still in pits are nobody's: only the stores count.

### Oware

- Four seeds in every pit. The stores are not part of the sowing; they only hold what you capture.
- A pit with twelve or more seeds skips itself on the way round.
- If your last seed makes a pit **on your opponent's side** hold two or three, you capture it, and the pit before it,
  and the one before that, as long as each holds two or three and is on their side.
- You may not capture every seed your opponent has: such a move is allowed but captures nothing.
- If your opponent's row is empty, you must sow into it if you can. If you can't, you keep the seeds on your side and
  the game ends.
- More than half the seeds, 25, wins; 24 each is a draw. If the same position keeps coming round, the game ends and
  each side keeps the seeds on its own side.

### Kalah

- Four seeds in every pit (three to six in Game settings).
- The sowing goes into **your** store but skips your opponent's.
- Every time your last seed lands in your store you sow again, as often as you can manage it.
- If your last seed lands in an **empty pit of your own** and the pit across has seeds, you capture both.
- When either row is empty, each side sweeps the seeds on its own side into its own store. Most seeds wins.

## Modes

| Mode | What |
|---|---|
| **Play the Learner** | 1978 Awari against the 1978 program's mind. It looks one move ahead and keeps a book of every game it fails to win. While a new game follows the opening of a lost one, it takes two points off any move that would repeat it, and you see it happen: the book opens, a page glows, and the old move drifts over the board as a ghost. |
| **Choose rules and opponent** | Any rule set against the Scholar (Easy, Normal or Hard), or against a friend on the same screen. The Learner plays only 1978 Awari. |
| **Daily Endgame** | One position a day, the same for everyone: "win in N" (finish ahead within N of your own sowings) or "win by K". The defence never makes a mistake. |
| **1970s mode** | The 1978 program on a terminal: type a pit, read the board in numbers. |

The first time you play each rule set, a card shows its rules.

## Settings

In **Game settings** on the game menu:

- **Rules to start with** and **Seeds a pit in Kalah**.
- **Show where a pit's seeds will go**: the dots and rings when you point at a pit.
- **Numbers by the pits**: off by default.
- **Sowing speed**: relaxed, normal or brisk.
- **The Learner's memory**: *keeps its book* between visits, or *as in 1978*, forgetting everything when you leave.
- **Open the Memory Book**, where you can also wipe it, and **Forget my data**.

Sound, light and dark, and motion are the collection's own settings, in the pause menu. With reduced motion, seeds
land in place without flying.

## Scoring

A game is won by the stores: yours against your opponent's at the end. The result card shows both, and against the
Learner it says whether the game went into its book. Records keeps wins, draws and losses for every rule set and
opponent.

## Daily

A new position at your local midnight. Days take turns between 1978 Awari and Kalah, and between "win in N" and
"win by K". If the goal slips out of reach (the solver checks after every move), you can try again straight away; the
first finish of the day is the one that counts. The share text gives the number, the rules, the goal, how many moves
you took, and one to three chestnuts: three for solving it on the first try.

## Tips

1. Count before you sow: in 1978 Awari and Kalah, a pit whose seeds exactly reach your store gives you a free move.
   Play those first, nearest the store first.
2. An empty pit on your side with a full pit across is a trap waiting to be sprung, for you or against you.
3. The Learner looks only one move ahead. Set up a capture two moves away and it will rarely see it coming.
4. Beat the Learner, then play the same opening again: watch where it turns away, and how long your trick lasts.
5. In Oware, keep a pit growing on your side. A big lap round the board can make twos and threes all along their row.
