# Outta Here: how to play

You are the star, an asterisk with a face, in the top-left corner of a 64 × 16 screen. Get to the **+** on the bottom row. Blocks rain in all the time, and much harder when you stand still: the walls grow while you hesitate. Pick a mode on the game menu with its letter key or a click. The screen appears frozen until your first move, so you can study it first: the clock, the rain and the chasers all start when you do.

## Controls

As in every Any Key game, `Esc` pauses and resumes, `?` lists the shortcuts, and `K` opens the Keyring from the game menu or a results card. These are the keys Outta Here adds.

### On the game menu

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `R` | the Run card | One screen on the difficulty picked below the cards |
| `S` | the Screens card | The map of thirty screens on five old computers |
| `D` | the Daily Escape card | Today's screen, the same for everyone |
| `V` | the Versus card | Two players on one keyboard |
| `1` | the 1980 mode card | The original program, bugs and all |
| | Chill, Normal or Hard | The difficulty for Run and Versus. The game remembers it |

On the Screens map, click a screen to play it. `Esc` or the Game menu button takes you back to the game menu.

### During a run (Run, Screens and the Daily)

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Arrow keys, or `W` `A` `S` `D` | the arrow pad (on touch screens) | Move one cell. Hold to keep moving |
| `,` and `.` | | Left and right, as in 1980 |
| `Space` or `B` | the Bomb button (on touch screens) | Drop a bomb where you stand |
| `M` or `E` | the Mega button (on touch screens) | Drop the Mega bomb you carry (Normal and Hard) |
| `P` | the Pause button in the top bar | Pause. `P` again resumes |
| `R` | Again | Start this screen over, straight away |
| `N` | New screen | A fresh screen (Run only) |
| | Game menu | Back to the game menu. If the run has started, it asks first |

### Paused

The pause menu reads, top to bottom: **Resume** (`Esc`), **Try again** (`R`), **New screen** (`N`, Run only) or **Screens map** (Screens only), **How to play**, **Settings**, **Game menu** and **Back to the Hall**. While it is open, `P` also resumes, `R` starts the screen over and `N` rolls a new Run screen.

Leaving a run that has started always asks first, with **Keep playing** and **Leave**. Browser Back asks the same question.

### When a run ends

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|

| `Enter` | Next screen | Screens only, after you get out (not after the very last screen) |
| `N` | New screen | Run only: a fresh screen |
| | Screens map | Screens only: back to the map |
| | Copy to share | Daily only: copies today's result (on a phone, opens the share sheet) |
| `Esc` | Game menu | Back to the game menu |
| `H` | Back to the Hall | Back to the collection |

### 1980 mode

| Key | Mouse or touch | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `Enter` | RUN (on touch screens) | Runs the program |
| `,` or ← | `,` | One step left |
| `.` or → | `.` | One step right |
| ↑ (or `[`) | ↑ | One step up |
| ↓ | ↓ | One step down |
| Any other key | any key | Nothing, except that it clears a block that has landed on you |
| `R` or `Enter` | Play again, or Switch it off and on | After the program ends or crashes |
| `H` | Back to the Hall | After the program ends or crashes |

Keys don't repeat, as on the TRS-80: one press, one step. `Esc` pauses the program (its clock stops with it); while paused, `R` runs it again. `P`, `B` and `Space` are just "any other key" here.

## The rules

### Getting out

Start in the top-left corner and step onto the **+** on the bottom row. On The Last Machine, the final computer of the Screens, there is no +: the way out is the cracked bottom-left corner.

The screen starts with stone walls running across it, each with a gap, so the way down zigzags. A few loose stones lie about, and some walls already have glass in them: there is always a way down that bombs can open. Everything else arrives with the rain.

### The rain and hesitation

- Blocks fall all the time. A dashed square shows where the next one will land about half a second before it does, and its colour says what is coming: wood, glass, stone or a bonus.
- Keep moving and the rain stays light. Stand still and the **Hesitation** meter fills, and the rain gets heavier: at a full meter it is about three times as heavy.
- While you keep moving, nothing lands in the eight cells around you. Stand still for 2.5 seconds on Normal, or 1.5 seconds on Hard, and blocks may land right next to you. On Chill they never do.
- On Hard a block can land on you. A dashed square on your own cell costs a heart if you are still there when it lands: step off it.
- On Chill the bottom row stays clear.

### Blocks

- **Wood** slides one cell when you walk into it, if the cell beyond is empty. It won't slide onto the exit, a letter, a bonus, a bomb or a chaser, or through an edge tunnel, and on Chill it won't slide onto the bottom row.
- **Glass** breaks when a bomb goes off next to it.
- **Stone** stays put. Go round, or break it with a Mega bomb (see below).

### The way out is never shut

The rain can't seal you in. If a block would cut your last way out, it lands as glass instead, and if even glass would cut it, it shatters as it lands. Wood won't slide if the push would box you in. So when you feel trapped, look for glass and bomb it. If you haven't moved for eight seconds, the game suggests `R` in case you'd rather start over.

### Bombs

- `Space` drops a bomb where you stand. It goes off 2.5 seconds later.
- The blast covers the 3 × 3 square around the bomb. It breaks the glass there, sets off any other bomb in it, pops any chaser in it (the chaser vanishes for six seconds, then comes back where it started), and hurts you if you are in it.
- One bomb on the screen at a time, two on Chill. The **Bomb** counter shows how many you can still drop. You can't drop one under the keys, and you can't walk onto one.

### Bonuses

**Chain ×3** and **Chain Rush** fall with the rain now and then. Walk onto one to pick it up; it fades eight seconds after it lands.

A chain bomb's blast doesn't stop at its 3 × 3 square: it runs on through every glass block touching one it broke, then every glass block touching those, and so on.

- **Chain ×3** makes your next three bombs chain bombs. Picking up another refills it to three; it doesn't stack.
- **Chain Rush** makes every bomb a chain bomb for 30 seconds. A bomb dropped during the rush still chains if it goes off after.

### The Mega bomb

On Normal and Hard (and so in the Daily Escape) one **Mega bomb** waits somewhere on the screen: a red bomb with a crack across it, sitting on a dashed ring. It never falls and never fades. You can always reach it: if the rain or a push walls it in, it moves somewhere you can get to.

- Walk onto it to pick it up. You carry one at a time: with one in hand, the next one stays where it is, drawn faded, until you have used yours. While you carry it, a little red bomb rides on your star's shoulder and the **Mega** counter reads 1.
- The moment you take it, another one appears somewhere else, a good walk away.
- `M` (or `E`) drops it where you stand. It goes off like a bomb, 2.5 seconds later, over the same 3 × 3 square, and it hurts you the same if you are in it. But it breaks **every** block there: wood, glass and stone, the stone walls included. It never chains.
- It doesn't use up your regular bomb: you can have both ticking at once.

### Letters

The letters **E S C A P E** sit on the screen from the start, spread from left to right. Walk over them to collect them, in any order. They are optional: all six earn the third star.

### Chasers

| Chaser | Looks like | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Bouncer** | a bouncing blue ball | Patrols its own short lane, back and forth, turning at the ends and at anything in its way. Time your crossing |
| **Eraser** | a pink rubber eraser | Waits a few seconds, then comes for your trail and follows it step by step, wiping it, even through an edge tunnel. If a block cuts the trail, it finds it again further on |
| **Glitch** | a jittering colour blob | Hunts you by the shortest way. When you hide under the keys it loses you and heads for where it last saw you |
| **Copycat** | a cat: black on the light screen, white on the dark | Hard only. It sets off a few seconds after you, from the corner where you started, and comes after you with your own tools: it pushes wood that is in its way and bombs glass, by exactly your rules. It is quick, so don't dawdle |

Chasers never walk through blocks, and none of them can follow you under the keys. Bouncers, Glitch and the Copycat never take an edge tunnel.

The Copycat never acts without warning. Before a push it leans into the wood for a moment, and two arrows on the wood show which way it will slide. Before a bomb it holds the bomb up over its head for three quarters of a second while its blast is marked out round it; then it drops it and backs off. Its bombs are yours with two little ears on, and a dashed ring for a fuse. It bombs you only when you are in its blast but out of its reach, behind glass say, and only when you have room to step clear. Like the other chasers it doesn't see your bombs coming, and it can't walk through one: a bomb dropped in a corridor stops it in its tracks.

When a chaser catches you, you lose a heart, your star blinks for two seconds while nothing can hurt it, and the chaser stops for a moment. The Eraser is also knocked back along your trail.

### Hearts

Chill has unlimited hearts, Normal 3, Hard 2, and every Screens screen 3. A catch, a block landing on you (Hard only), your own bomb and the Copycat's each cost one. Lose the last and the run is over: the card says **Out of hearts** and what got you.

### Tricks from 1980

The 1980 program had bugs. Here they are tricks.

- **Edge tunnels.** Walk off the right edge of the screen and you come in at the left end of the next row down. Walk off the left edge and you come in at the right end of the row above. Dashed seams on both sides of the screen mark them. There is no tunnel left from the top row or right from the bottom row.
- **Under the keys.** Walk up off the top row and you hide under the keyboard drawn above the screen. Walk left and right along it, and press down to drop back onto an empty cell of the top row. Nothing can catch or hit you there, but the clock keeps running, the rain keeps falling, and standing still fills the meter as usual.

## Modes

### Run

One screen on the difficulty you pick on the game menu. **Again** (`R`) replays the same screen; **New screen** (`N`) rolls another. The title shows the screen's number.

| | Chill | Normal | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it's for | Learning the moves | The game as designed | A real fight |
| Rain while moving | light | steady | heavier |
| Blocks next to you after standing still | never | 2.5 s | 1.5 s |
| Blocks can land on you | no | no | yes |
| Bottom row | stays clear | can close | can close |
| Hearts | unlimited | 3 | 2 |
| Bombs on screen | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| Chasers | none | 3 Bouncers and the Eraser | 4 Bouncers, the Eraser, Glitch and the Copycat |
| Mega bomb | none | one on the screen | one on the screen |
| Par | 18 s | 45 s | 50 s |

### Screens

Thirty fixed screens on five old computers, six each, the same for everyone. Each computer has its own look and teaches one trick. Screen 1 is open from the start; each screen opens when you clear the one before it, so each computer switches on when you clear the last screen of the one before. Every screen has three hearts and its own par, and the rain gets a little heavier through each computer's six screens.

| Computer | Look | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Green Screen** | green phosphor | The rain and the Bouncers, on two to four floors. Learn to bomb glass and push wood |
| **Amber Terminal** | amber glow | The gaps sit at the very ends of the walls, so the edge tunnels are the quick way down. The Eraser joins |
| **Home Micro** | eight colours | Stone pillars cut the top corridor. The way past is under the keys. Glitch joins |
| **Pocket LCD** | grey-green LCD | Sand: every block slides down its column, through gaps too, and piles up. Stay high and move fast |
| **The Last Machine** | magenta and cyan | No exit. Get to the bottom-left corner, where the 1980 program crashed the computer. Glitch from screen 3, blocks that can land on you from screen 4 |

The map shows your stars on every screen, out of 90 in all. The Screens have no Mega bomb and no Copycat: each one plays exactly as it did when it was picked.

### Daily Escape

One Normal screen a day, the same for everyone. See *Daily* below.

### Versus

Two players on one keyboard, side by side, on the difficulty picked for Run. Each player gets their own copy of the same screen, so the rain and the chasers only react to what that player does. After a 3, 2, 1 countdown, the first one out wins. Lose all your hearts and the other player wins; if both go down at the same moment, it's a draw. On Chill nobody runs out of hearts, so it's a pure race. Versus doesn't count towards your stats or keycaps.

### 1980 mode

The original program, run step for step on a TRS-80-sized screen: white on black, 64 × 16 characters, no colours and no score. The screen opens at BASIC's prompt with `RUN` typed: press `Enter`. Then reach the + with comma, full stop and the arrows, while a block falls every moment you aren't pressing a key, about sixty a second.

Everything the 1980 program got wrong still works here. Walk off a row's end and you come in on the next row. Step right from the very first cell and nothing happens. Walk up off the top row and the star vanishes into the keyboard's memory. And a certain corner is best left alone, unless you want to find out what happened to the computer. The line under the screen tells you where your star is, because the screen can't.

Reach the + and the program simply stops at `READY`, as it did in 1980.

## Settings

Settings opens from the pause menu, and also lives on the Keyring page. It holds:

- **Sound** on or off, and the **Volume**. The sound button in the top bar mutes everything at once.
- **Appearance**: Light, Dark or System. Run, the Daily and Versus switch with it straight away; the five Screens computers and 1980 mode keep their own screens.
- **Motion**: Follow my device, Less motion or Full motion. It calms the menus and the cards; the game screen itself calms its pulsing and wobbling when your device asks for reduced motion.

Outta Here's own setting is the difficulty, picked on the game menu. It sets Run and Versus, and the game remembers it.

## Scoring

### Stars

Every run can earn three stars:

1. **Got out**: you reached the exit.
2. **Under par**: you got out within the par time, shown next to the clock. Run's par is 18 s on Chill, 45 s on Normal and 50 s on Hard; the Daily uses Normal's; each Screens screen has its own, between 20 and 80 seconds.
3. **E-S-C-A-P-E**: you collected all six letters. The card shows this one even if you were caught.

### Score

The results card adds up:

- 1,000 for getting out,
- 20 for every second under twice the par,
- 100 for each letter,
- 5 for each glass block your bombs broke (the Copycat's don't count),
- 10 for each glass block your best chain blast broke,
- and takes away 150 for each hit.

A run that ends in a catch scores only its letters and its glass. The card also shows your time, your steps, the stone you broke (if any) and a little map of the way you went.

### What the game remembers

On this browser, Outta Here keeps your number of runs and escapes, your best time and best score on each difficulty (the game menu shows the best times), the best stars and time on every Screens screen, and the first result of every Daily. Only finished runs count; a run you restart or leave isn't recorded. Versus and 1980 mode aren't counted.

## Daily

- A new Daily Escape starts at your own midnight. They are numbered from 1 January 2026, which was #1, so 7 October 2026 is #280.
- Everyone gets the same screen on the same day, with Normal rules: 3 hearts, one bomb at a time, a Mega bomb to find, three Bouncers and the Eraser, par 45 s.
- The first run you finish each day is the one that counts. After that you can keep playing it as practice, and the card says so.
- The game menu shows today's number and how it went: not tried yet, out in so many seconds, or caught.
- Finishing the Daily, out or caught, marks today as done on the Keyring and keeps your daily streak going.

The share text is three lines: the game and the Daily's number; your time (or "caught"), your stars as filled and hollow stars, and your hits; then ten coloured squares and a plus if you got out or a cross if you were caught. Each square stands for a tenth of your run and shows its most eventful second: red if you were hurt, orange if a bomb went off, blue if you were under the keys, yellow if you were hesitating, green if you kept moving. **Copy to share** copies it on a computer, and opens the share sheet on a phone.

## Tips

1. **Look before you move.** Nothing happens until your first step, so find the gaps and the glass first.
2. **Keep moving, even sideways.** The rain stays light, and nothing lands next to you while you move.
3. **Glass is a door.** Drop a bomb next to it, step two cells away, and wait for the bang. Stepping one cell away is not enough.
4. **Take the tunnels.** When a wall's gap is at the far end, walking off the edge of the screen is often the quickest way down.
5. **Hide under the keys.** No chaser can follow you there. It's the way past Glitch, and past the pillars on the Home Micro.
6. **Turn the Eraser round.** It only follows your trail: lead it into a bomb blast and it's gone for six seconds.
7. **Save Chain ×3 for a big patch of glass.** One chain blast through fifteen glass blocks earns Chain Reaction, and every block is worth points.
8. **Spend the Mega bomb on a wall.** Standing just above a stone wall, it blows a three-wide hole straight down: a whole corridor saved. Fetch it when it is near your way; the next one is always a walk off.
9. **Plug a corridor for the Copycat.** It can't walk through a bomb and doesn't see the blast coming: drop one where it will come down after you, step clear, and it may walk right up to it.
